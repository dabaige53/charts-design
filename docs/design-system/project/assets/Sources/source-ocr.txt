## Elevation on MDS

Elevations represent di!erent user interface layers simulated through the use of shadows. Placing di!erent elements on di!erent layers creates a clear hierarchy of interface elements and makes it easier to locate currently available options for the user. Layers are represented by various sized shadows to indicate a position along an imaginary z-axis extending above the user interface towards the user.

## 0px elevation

Content and common UI elements are usually placed on the surface of the page at the 0px elevation. 

## 4px elevation

The simplest components requiring contrast and the user's attention, such as tooltips and popovers, are placed at the 4px elevation. 

## 16px elevation

Components that require the most user focus like drawers, modals, and pickers are place at the upper most layer. 

## 2px elevation

The 2px layer elevation helps to indicate interactive components. After interaction, the component may rise to another elevation layer. 

## 8px elevation

More complex components and items with interactions such as navigation menus and cards in a hover state are placed at the 8px elevation. 

## Spacing

Spacing is one of the most important aspects of visual design and is foundational to the McKinsey Design System. Underlying every layout, component or spatial relationship is a 4pt spacing system. We use spacing consistently and intentionally to establish order, hierarchy and rhythm. 

## Spacing sizes and increments

All spacing helpers are percentages or multiples of 4px (0.25rem). Using 4px as a constant o!ers "exibility, clean numbers to work with, and helps improve design and accessibility when fonts are scaled in a browser. This also o!ers spacing consistency and consistent rhythm throughout the design system. 

## Types of spacing

Use margins and padding to create several types of spacing. See examples below for Inset spacing, Inline spacing, Stacked spacing, Spacing text, and Margins.

## Spacing text

Avoid measuring to the baseline of text. That creates unexpected spacing results. 

Space text to the direct selection of a text box in Sketch. Line height determines text box size, so apply the correct line height to the text before making measurements.

## 4pt grid system guidance

The 4pt grid system is the underlying fabric that provides consistency and structure to all visual elements of McKinsey's Design System. It establishes a framework for consistently scaling and evolving the design system in a predictable and !exible way over time. All spacing, typography, and visual elements adhere to the 4pt grid system. 

## Getting started

The artboards you see here are setup with 4pt grid and layout settings in a variety of common sizes used for designing responsive web applications. These can be used as is or as a starting point for your projects. Typically Designers will choose three artboards representing mobile, tablet and desktop. The 4pt grid is very !exible and you are encouraged to setup your artboard to suit your needs using the grid system.

## Breadcrumbs

Breadcrumbs are used to help the users keep track and maintain awareness of the overall locations within a speci!c navigation that the user is currently in. A breadcrumb must have the minimum size of 2 items and the maximum of 5 items, so that it’s easier for the user to use it.

Although card dimensions may vary a lot depending on the content they have, the general rule is to match the size of other nearby cards - for example, matching the height and width of cards in the same row and column. This will reinforce a grid structure and help the user identify and categorize the content into imaginary boxes for quick scanning and focus. The 16px spacing between the call to action and the penultimate content should be considered the minimum and allowed to vary depending on the height of the card as dictated by other nearby cards and the height of the card's content.

## Modals

A modal is a content container that !oats above a main page layout and it is triggered by a click event on that page. When active, it blocks all other page interactions, allowing users to focus on smaller pieces of critical content or perform an activity that requires their full attention. Modals are invasive to the user's work!ow as they block all other content and allows them to focus on a single task. A deep blue layer (#051C2C) with 85% opacity stands between the page and the modal and can be dismissed by selecting the close (X) icon, a CTA (e.g. Cancel, Submit) and in some instances depending on the context, by clicking anywhere on the opacity layer layer. 


Speci!cations/Anatomy


![image](https://cdn-mineru.openxlab.org.cn/result/2026-11-10/f82eadb1-353c-49f2-9c72-b6e4eb420a1c/dd535ff69820fcc1d35fe6ae7d8ee5913ca4e193d6341ce8d85c7e09b01a7615.jpg)


A modal is made up of 3 sections, the top section, the content section and the bottom section. All sections are optional and should be determined by the needs of the solution. 

The Top Section may contain a representative icon to help communicate the message or give a sense of status or urgency to the action being requested. The top section also include an "X" icon to dismiss the modal. The graphic is not always required but recommended. 

The Content Section contains a title, text, data visualizations, input #elds, and any other variety of media, including photos, graphics or illustrations. All content should be written succinctly and objectively so that it occupies as few lines as possible. 

The Bottom Section contains action items including links and buttons to proceed. Typical combinations may include Cancel   |   Delete   |   Save   | Send. 

Modals can have many di$erent sizes but must always maintain the 40px padding on the top and bottom margins and should never consume more than 50% of a screen. 

On mobile viewports, modals typically take up more space and have larger CTAs to make them tap friendly.
